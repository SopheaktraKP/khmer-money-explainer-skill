# Script formula

## Structure of an 8-10 minute video

| Part | Length | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 20-30 s | Two people, same starting point, opposite results, in real dollars. End on the question. No greeting and no channel intro. |
| The setup | 45-60 s | Who they are, the fork, what each one believes. Everything concrete: their job, their street, their amounts. |
| How it works | 1-2 min | Explain the mechanism with one running example. Assume the viewer has never heard the jargon. |
| Option A: the good side | 1-1.5 min | The honest case for it, with arithmetic. |
| Option A: the catch | 1-1.5 min | Costs the salesperson doesn't mention. |
| Option B: both sides | 1.5-2 min | Same treatment, so neither feels like the "right" answer too early. |
| The result | 1 min | Time-skip, then the totals side by side. Show at least 2-3 scenarios, because the answer usually changes with an assumption. |
| The risks | 1 min | What can go wrong on each path, and how likely, using reported data. |
| Before you decide | 45 s | 3-5 questions viewers ask themselves. Not "you should do X". |
| Close | 15-20 s | Call back to the hook. Ask which person the viewer is, and invite a comment. |

## Line-by-line habits

- **One idea per beat, one number per beat.** If a sentence holds three figures, split it and show them one at a time.
- **Say what the number means.** Not only "$397 is interest" but "out of $486, only $89 actually pays for the house."
- **Don't describe what's on screen; add to it.** The picture shows the jar filling, while the voice says why that matters.
- **Use round, memorable example amounts** that suit local incomes: $100 spare a month, a $400 salary. State them as examples.
- **Use a small human moment every minute or so:** a mother's phone call, Khmer New Year spending, a colleague's new phone. It keeps long videos watchable.
- **Keep the tone calm and friendly,** like a smart older sibling. Never mock either character. Avoid get-rich language and avoid fear-mongering.
- **Plant the callback in the hook** and pay it off at the end.

## Khmer writing

- Prefer plain spoken Khmer over formal or translated phrasing. If a term is commonly said in English (franchise, Subscribe), the spoken English word is fine.
- Explain a technical word the first time it appears, then use it freely.
- Write money with digits and the word for dollars ("88 ដុល្លារ") in narration. KiriTTS reads digits naturally. Add a `tts` spelling only for a specific misread.
- On screen, use `$` and Latin digits for amounts, which matches how prices appear in Cambodia.
- Pick ordinary Khmer first names, and don't reuse names strongly tied to another creator's characters.
- Sentence ends use `។`. Keep commas rare and use a space for a spoken pause, as Khmer writing normally does.
- The model's Khmer needs a human reader. Ask the user to read the script before voicing, and take their word on terminology.

## Topic bank

- Tontine (តុង) vs bank
- Gold vs fixed deposit
- New moto: instalment vs saving up and paying cash
- Work abroad in Korea vs stay in Phnom Penh: five-year totals
- Land in the province vs fixed deposit
- Stay at a job vs start a small business, with real stall costs
- Microfinance loan vs family loan: the real price of each
- Used car vs tuk-tuk or ride-hailing costs for a year
- Wedding budget: a big wedding vs a house deposit
- Buy now pay later: what "0%" really costs
